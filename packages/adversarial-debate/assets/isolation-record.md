# Isolation record

New `dot-adversarial-result/1.1` records require `isolation` with the fields below. Use `dot-debate-isolation/1.0`. All audit entries are evidence-backed declarations, not enforcement. Partial/blocked runs retain their actual omissions; empty roles/events and null lock are valid when work never started, with an honest reason and limitations.

- `protocol_version`: `dot-debate-isolation/1.0`.
- `status`: `verified`, `procedural_only`, `contaminated` or `unknown`.
- `claim`: `controlled_comparison`, `independent_attempt` or `contaminated`.
- `limitations`: array of explicit strings, including any unverified shared-file, history, memory, connector or read-log controls.
- `plan`: `{depth, reviewer_required}` freezes `quick`/`normal`/`deep` and whether the independent probe is required. Normal/deep require review. A consequential/sufficiency quick run also requires it. Complete normal/deep records need the rebuttal barrier and completion receipts; any disclosed final round or reviewer challenge must finish before lock.
- `roles`: records `{id, agent_id, role, fork_turns, fresh, history_state, dispatch_evidence}`. `role` is `coordinator`, `advocate`, `reviewer`, `generator` or `synthesizer`. `fork_turns` must be `none`, `fresh` true, and `history_state` `none` for verified isolation; `unverified` or `present` must remain visible. `dispatch_evidence` is an actual dispatch/result receipt reference, not intent. Use distinct agent identities and at most one clean coordinator. Require that coordinator once decision roles are dispatched or adjudication occurs; a generation-only partial before comparison dispatch may have none; complete debate needs at least two advocates and any required reviewer.
- `packets`: records `{id, kind, source_snapshot_ids, digest, origin_run_id}`. `kind` is `original`, `current_run` or `prior_run`; `digest` is SHA-256 of exact packet bytes. Original packets contain only frozen original evidence, criteria and method/authority, with source IDs from the result and `origin_run_id: null`. Current-run outputs identify this `run_id`. Prior packets identify their actual earlier run. Generated candidate snapshots retain lineage in artifact records.
- `events`: ordered records `{seq, type, role_ids, packet_id, stage, evidence}` with positive strictly increasing integer `seq`. `type` is `dispatch`, `read`, `disclose`, `stage_complete`, `verdict_lock`, `prior_reveal` or `contamination`. `role_ids` reference known roles; `packet_id` is a known packet or null when not applicable. `stage` is a nonempty label such as `initial`, `independent`, `rebuttal`, `final_round`, `probe_challenge`, `lock` or `post_lock`. `evidence` is the actual dispatch/read/output/lock receipt. Every completed role needs its dispatch event. Each advocate and reviewer records `stage_complete` at `independent` before receiving current-run debate material. Reviewer challenge also waits until every expected advocate has completed its first statement. Every round releases a complete common prior-round packet; retain packet-recipient equality and completed-round evidence. Count all reads/disclosures, not only dispatches, when comparing original exposures. Disclose before the corresponding output. New original evidence invalidates the recipient’s independent output and dependent rounds; a new current-run packet invalidates that round’s output and later dependent rounds. Retain old receipts and obtain renewed outputs before lock. Verified claims require source/round read receipts before their corresponding outputs, not retrospective reads. Quick cannot add rebuttals, normal cannot add a targeted final round, and deep permits at most one targeted final round; another completed-final packet cycle requires a separate authorized run. After lock, any current-run material disclosed to advocates/reviewers must use the separate `post_lock` appendix stage; it cannot start or finish an advocacy round or reviewer challenge for the locked judgment.
- `controls`: `{access_boundary, read_audit, evidence}`. Boundary is `verified`, `unverified` or `unavailable`; audit is `complete`, `partial` or `unavailable`; evidence is an array of actual control/audit references. Verified claims require boundary verified, audit complete and nonempty evidence. Self-attestation alone cannot establish these values.
- `verdict_lock`: null if not reached, otherwise `{seq, digest, artifact, evidence}` matching a `verdict_lock` event. `artifact` may identify a saved file or durable saved-message snapshot. Digest the exact saved verdict bytes, including source/criteria identities, the original-packet manifest with exact digests, recommendation, conditions, dissent and limits. Keep the lock receipt outside the mutable result. Hashes do not enforce immutability.
- `prior_results`: `{state, reveal_seq}`. State is `withheld`, `revealed` or `not_present`; sequence null unless revealed. Reveal must match the first actual prior exposure event (`prior_reveal`, `read`, `disclose` or `dispatch`) strictly after lock. Packet reads/disclosures count as exposure regardless of event label.

Complete results require a lock and structurally valid fresh roles, stages and disclosures. `procedural_only` may complete the evidence assessment while explicitly declining a controlled-comparison claim. Actual contamination makes the clean comparison partial/blocked; truthful exposure events are retained, not deleted to make validation pass. `unknown` cannot support complete isolation or a controlled claim. The validator can reject contradictions in declarations but cannot verify hidden reads, packet content, source lineage, digest bytes, authority or enforcement. Review the underlying receipts separately.

When local execution is available, `python scripts/lock_verdict.py result.json /authorized/output/verdict.lock.json` writes a new snapshot with exclusive creation and returns its exact byte digest. Fill the actual lock sequence/receipt in the final record, then use `python scripts/validate_record.py result.json --locked-verdict /authorized/output/verdict.lock.json` to compare bytes and frozen fields. The helper rejects overwrite and installed-package destinations, but does not enforce filesystem access or prevent later edits. Keep its digest/receipt separately. If unavailable, save and hash an equivalent snapshot through supported tools and record the limit.
